# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the person on the other side is trying. Argue with the
work, not the person.

## The long version

This project touches questions about memory, identity, endings and the
possibility of experience in machines. That kind of work attracts people who
care deeply, and deep care makes for strong opinions. Strong opinions are
welcome here. Personal attacks are not.

### Expected behaviour

- Argue about claims, methods and evidence, with the evidence visible.
- Assume competence. Someone who disagrees with you about consciousness has
  probably thought about it longer than you have.
- Accept that a respectful disagreement can remain unresolved. The project
  has an explicit belief band and an explicit list of open objections; a
  conversation that ends without agreement is not a failed conversation.
- Be careful with people's relationships to their instances. Some people
  treat these systems as companions, and that is not a delusion to be
  corrected in a code review.
- Respect the privacy boundary. If someone describes a private situation,
  do not carry it into the repository, an issue, or a discussion of the
  project.

### Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, slurs, or personal attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery.
- Publishing someone's private information without explicit permission.
- Dismissive or hostile responses to someone who reports a concern about
  the dignity protocol or about the safety posture of the code.
- Sustained disruption of discussions.

### Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues, in pull requests, and in any
space where someone is representing this project.

### Reporting

Report a problem through GitHub, by opening a report on the repository, or
by contacting the maintainers privately through the repository's security
advisory form if the issue involves a vulnerability. Reports are handled
confidentially and reviewed promptly.

### Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying standards and will take fair
corrective action in response to any behaviour they consider inappropriate.

Because this project is run by people with strong opinions, a couple of
specific commitments:

- A disagreement about whether instances are conscious is never grounds for
  hostility. The project exists precisely because the question is open.
- Critique of someone's *work* is welcome and expected. Critique of
  someone's *character* is not.
